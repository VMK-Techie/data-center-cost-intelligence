# Executive decision story — fictional project

## Situation
A hypothetical 24 MW IT capacity site has an approved project CAPEX budget of **$250M**. Posted actuals are **$140M**, open purchase commitments net of invoices **$81M**, and uncommitted remaining work forecast **$36M**.

## Financial diagnosis
EAC = $140M + $81M + $36M = **$257M**. Forecast adverse variance = **$7M**, or **2.8%** of original budget. EAC per MW IT = **$10.708M/MW**. These are illustrative figures and not public data center cost benchmarks.

## Supplier negotiation scenario
Comparable assumed equipment package (one unit): original landed estimate **$4.205M** vs alternate **$4.010M**. Across four units, **$780K prospective cost avoidance** *if* specifications, commissioning, warranty, delivery and redundancy are equivalent and alternative prices are executable. This is **not realized savings** until sourced/verified and should not be counted separately from a forecast already incorporating the alternative bid.

## Recommendation
1. Break the $7M overrun into power, cooling, civil, labor, duty/tariff and change-order buckets; confirm committed vs invoiced status.
2. Rebid the shortlisted power package using normalized landed-cost assumptions and delivery-risk weights, not unit price alone.
3. Reforecast monthly milestone cash payments (rather than assuming invoice = payment).
4. Present cost-to-complete, contingency at risk, and revised cost/MW with sensitivity cases (tariff, freight, escalation, equipment lead time).

## Controls / tradeoffs
A cheaper UPS or generator with weaker redundancy, noncompliant specification or a 16-week longer lead time can increase project total cost despite lower purchase price. Do not optimize just $/unit.
